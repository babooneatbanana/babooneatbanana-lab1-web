Data: 
The data that I present in my map include the location of all public schools in the City of Vancouver and City of Vancouver bikeways. Bikeway data is classified into four categories: 1) protected bikeways, 2) local street bikeways, 3) painted bikeways, and 4) shared bikeways. All data was available in GeoJSON format from the Vancouver Open Data Portal

Reflective analysis:
This map was designed for City of Vancouver policy makers and cycling enthusiast to provoke thought about the connection between cycling infrastructure and public schools. Public schools across Vancouver have initiatives encourage children to cycle and use active transportation to get to work. However, cycling infrastructure often lacks the safety focus needed to parents to feel safe letting their kids cycle to school. As a result, schools throughout Vancouver are often disconnected from the safest type of bikeways, 'protected bikeways', and instead have to use often types of bikeways that are not as safe. This lack of safety undermines active transportation initiatives at public schools. Therefore, using this map, planners and policy makers can build and improve cycling infrastructure while considering the connectivity of public schools to bikeways. 

Regarding the base map, I stylized it in Maputnik to be as basic as possible. Roads were classified and given hierarchy with different shades of gray and all green spaces were given a dull green color. This way the base map provides little confusion to the viewer but it still informs the viewer about key transportation infrastructure. 

The bikeways were assigned colors red, orange, yellow, and green indicating least safe (shared lanes) in red to most safe (protected lanes) in green. Protected lanes and share lanes were also given a thicker stroke to make the safest and least safe lanes pop for the viewer. This way it is communicated were cycling infrastructure is adequate for safety and where it is woefully inadequate. Public Schools were assigned a purple to clearly differentiate them from bikeway elements. To build in some interactivity, a pop-up feature was added that allows the user to click on a school and discovery its name. This way, users can understand which areas around schools (by name) might be in need of improved cycling infrastructure.

A small but informative title was added on the top left-hand corner of the screen to help inform the user about what they were looking at. 

The map could have been improved in a few ways. Firstly, it does not clearly communicate which schools are near what type of cycling infrastructure. Since the ultimate goal of this assignment was to illustrate spatial interaction between data/phenomenon, schools could be classified and symbolized on the map based on their closest type of bikeway. For example, schools near protected bike lanes could be given a check mark and those far from protect lanes could have an 'x' filled in the middle. This way it would be clear which schools lack adequate cycling infrastructure and prompt the user to further consider the connections between public schools and cycling infrastructure. I did also try to have the circle radius increase as the user zooms in, but I was not able to figure out how functions would be calling on an already stylized circle. However, I still think I provided the basic information and layout necessary for a user to consider the connection between these two data points.
